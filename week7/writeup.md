## My comments vs. the AI's — per PR

**PR #1** — AI raised 2 "blockers" claiming `NotePatch` allows empty strings
because the fields are optional. **False positive**: `Field(default=None,
min_length=1)` validates any provided value; only omission is allowed (that's
the point of PATCH). Its warn about asserting error bodies and the nit about
`db.py` seeding were plausible but wrong for this codebase (`apply_seed_if_needed`
already checks emptiness — the AI couldn't see it in the diff). **My review
caught what it missed**: the `words` metric counts whitespace-joined CJK text as
one word (acceptable, documented).

**PR #2** — AI's findings were mostly style (type hints/docstrings it claimed
were missing — they exist on the public functions) and test-strength nits. Two
were legitimate: my `or`-assertion weakness (same one I'd flagged) and
suggesting a `None`-input test. **My review caught what it missed**: the
output-shape change for downstream consumers (breaking-change analysis), which
is the consequential risk of the PR.

**PR #3** — AI verdict approve; findings minor (a redundant `db.flush()`
claim — actually needed for the session lifecycle here; a CASCADE-vs-SET-NULL
opinion). **My review caught what it missed**: the get-or-create race on
concurrent tag attach (unique-constraint IntegrityError window) — a real
production concern I documented as out-of-scope.

**PR #4** — the interesting one. AI said **request-changes** with 2 "blockers":
(1) unknown sort should 422 — factually wrong, the route has a `hasattr`
fallback and returns 200 (test proved it); (2) my tautological `or`-assertions —
**right**, genuinely weak tests. I fixed (2), strengthened the filter test per
its suggestion, and the strengthened test then **exposed a real behavior**:
SQLite `ORDER BY title` is binary/case-sensitive, not case-insensitive — now
pinned and documented in the test. This is the pattern where AI review earned
its keep: not by being right about the code, but by being right about the test.

## When AI review was better / worse than mine

- **Better**: test-quality criticism (weak assertions, tautologies, missing
  edge cases) — it systematically enumerated them across every PR; I catch
  these unevenly. Also sheer coverage: 10-12 findings per diff, forcing me to
  re-verify claims I'd have skipped.
- **Worse**: severity calibration (2 false "blockers" from misreading optional
  fields) and codebase context (flagging seeding behavior it couldn't see,
  wrong claims about missing type hints). It also repeated near-duplicate
  findings (PR2 #11/#12).
- **Surprise**: the AI's wrong "blocker" still led to a real improvement —
  defending it forced me to write the proof into the test, which then surfaced
  the binary-sorting contract. Wrong finding, right outcome.

## Comfort level & heuristics going forward

I'd treat AI review as a **draft reviewer**: run it on every PR, triage every
blocker personally before acting, expect ~20-40% false positives on
severity/context, and trust it most on (a) test strength and (b) mechanical
invariants (validation constraints, unused code, missing error paths). Human
review stays mandatory for API-contract changes, data-model migrations, and
anything with security surface — exactly the categories where the AI's missing
codebase context produced its worst calls this session.
